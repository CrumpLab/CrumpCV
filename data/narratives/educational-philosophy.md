My educational philosophy can be summarized in two metaphors. First, I tell students that my teaching role is similar to a museum tour guide. The possible content that could be covered in many courses is large like a museum, where there is only so much time in a day to walk through it before getting too tired to go on. So, I become a tour guide of my course, teaching in detail about specific "artifacts", but also raising awareness about the scale of the topics, and the many possible avenues that students might pursue when the course is over. The second metaphor is the classroom as a carpentry workshop. Apprentices in a carpentry workshop learn hands-on skills that deliver tangible products (e.g., new kitchen cabinetry). There are many practical academic skills and I use the workshop metaphor to convince students that coming to class is going to the workshop, where they actively practice thinking skills like talking, writing, critically evaluating arguments, and practical skills like using computer software to create presentations, run experiments, and analyze data. My time at Brooklyn College has solidified these metaphors as useful approaches to education, and inspired a maxim to always have an answer to the question, "What new skill are you learning?". My students come from many walks of life, they are diligent and interested in their studies, and they consistently motivate me to build upon my skills as a researcher and teacher so that I have more resources to share with them.
